Course guide¶

Science, my lad, is made up of mistakes, but they are mistakes which it is useful to make, because they lead little by little to the truth.

– Jules Verne, A Journey to the Center of the Earth.

Distributed Systems is an optional subject of the Bachelor’s Degree in Informatics Engineering of the UPV/EHU, associated to the Computer Engineering specialty. In GAUR you can find the official Teaching Guide of the subject, where competencies and other formal aspects are defined.

Basically, a distributed system is a network system with some additional properties, so that this subject can be considered as an additional stage in your training in the specialty of Computer Engineering.

Respecting the objectives, competences and syllabus of the subject, in this course we will propose an approach based on the realization of a project in groups from the beginning of the course.

Do I meet the necessary requirements for the course?¶

The knowledge of computer networks, operating systems, software engineering and other disciplines, which you have acquired in the previous courses of the Degree in Informatics Engineering, you can apply here. In particular, the subject of Servicios y Aplicaciones en Red, of the third year of the degree can be considered as the closest reference for this subject.

What will I learn in the course?¶

As stated in the Teaching Guide, you will learn to propose and develop solutions on a computer network according to the criteria and properties of distributed systems, and to analyze and compare the performance of the solutions and mechanisms applied.

In addition, working as a team, you will learn to develop projects on distributed systems, to document solutions properly and to expose them in public, very important competencies in the professional world.

How is the course developed?¶

The contents of the syllabus defined in the Teaching Guide are structured in the course according to a sequence of activities that revolve fundamentally around a project, although some of the contents will not be integrated into the project for reasons of time.

We will work in groups of three students, which will be formed the first week of class.

The development of the subject in class follows the sequence of activities, grouped into several Parts as it appears in the sidebar of this Web. This implies continuous evaluation, as specified below. If for any reason you will not be able to attend class on an ongoing basis, talk to the teacher to plan a learning method that allows you to pass the subject through a unique evaluation, according to your personal situation. In that case, you can follow the course based on the Notes of the subject, which include exercises and follow the order established in the teaching guide. You can also rely on other bibliographic resources and, of course, on tutoring hours.

A couple of comments about the learning method¶

Keep in mind these two Basic Rules on which the method we are going to follow is based:

  1. Learning is a process, not a goal. Sometimes, particularly at the beginning of some activities, the teacher will ask you for works and reports whose objective you will not see clearly, and possibly the result will not leave you satisfied. A later explanation will allow you to see the mistakes you have made. Consider those mistakes as an important part of the learning process.

  2. As important as knowing what you have learned is knowing what you have not learned. We cannot pretend to cover everything, neither in this subject nor in the whole Degree. What you will learn is not unique. Simply the curricula are not perfect. That is why I do not intend that, after the course, you can say: I already know Distributed Systems, but rather: I already know what I have to learn to face the design and implementation of any distributed system. Therefore, in some works it is explicitly requested that you explain what you have left undone.

How much time should I devote?¶

The course is intended for a dedication of about 120 hours, including classes and work outside of them. As a general rule, you should spend almost as much time working outside of class as in class.

You will see that for each activity its estimated duration is specified at the beginning. Look at these estimates as an important reference, particularly in terms of time spent outside class. Go to the teacher in case of difficulties or in situations of blocking that could make you waste too much time, subtracting it from your dedication to other subjects.

What are the evaluation methods?¶

We intend that you take the form of continuous evaluation, which is the one that fits with the development of the subject throughout the classes. The continuous evaluation demands the attendance to the classes and a continuous attention to the subject. If you are not going to be able to follow the course in these conditions, you can do it based on the Notes of the subject and take advantage of the single evaluation. You can also consider abandoning the continuous evaluation at any time during the course, and in that case we would consider the particular conditions. However, keep in mind that, since the work in the subject is mainly in groups, the abandonment of the continuous evaluation would have an impact on your groupmates.

Continuous evaluation¶

The continuous evaluation of the subject is based on the presentation in time of a series of deliverables as results of the proposed activities, the realization of a set of tests and the presentation of the project’s memory of the subject:

  • Activity reports. Many activities are partially evaluated based on reports or small works to be delivered within the established deadlines. The evaluation of these reports is by group. Reports are not always written. For some activities the teacher will ask the group to explain the results orally. Although the teacher can be flexible with the deadlines, it is important to respect them, since the teacher will respond to your report with comments that could be useful to correct errors in the face of the next test.

  • Individual tests. They are performed at the end of a set of activities. They consist of short questions and small exercises, related to the work commissioned in the preceding activities, which require concise and reasoned answers.

  • Project. It is evaluated based on the quality of the memory delivered and the results obtained. The class exposure of the work done is also evaluated, which will include an individual component.

The Project is valued as 20% of the subject. The remaining 80% is divided between the tests (60%) and the reports (40%), although this distribution is not homogeneous throughout the activities.

In order to remain in the continuous evaluation, the fulfillment of the deadlines in all deliverables is essential (unless justified cause). You must bear in mind that the work in a deliverable will serve as learning for the activity test, and that if it is delivered in time the teacher will be able to send the comments and corrections to the group. In addition, a minimum test score is required:

  • You must reach at least 40% in the overall test score.

  • You cannot stay below 30% in more than 25% of the tests (according to their overall weight).

  • You cannot leave more than one test undone.

In some activities a self-assessment test is included in order to verify what you have learned. It has no impact on the evaluation of the subject.

Single evaluation¶

For those who do not follow the continuous evaluation or leave it, there will be an alternative final single evaluation test, which includes a Project (on a subject to be agreed with the teacher) and an exam.

Evaluation criteria¶

Criteria for qualifying reports¶

  • The solutions provided must be reasonably correct.

  • They must use the terms and concepts precisely.

  • They must be original and do not include literal content of other texts, be they books, notes or other works.

  • The report must be easy to understand and respect the formalities of language (spelling, syntax…).

  • The report must relate the results obtained with those of other activities.

  • The report must indicate alternative solutions and approaches.

Criteria for qualifying the Project (report and code)¶

In addition to the criteria used for the rest of the reports, the following will apply:

  • The code must respect the modularity proposed.

  • The code must be expressed in a clear and structured manner, and must be reasonably efficient.

  • The code must meet the functional requirements established.

  • The tests performed must show consistency with the objective pursued.

  • The verification must collect the test results with fidelity.

  • The structure of the report must allow us to understand what has been done and the results achieved.

  • The conclusions of the report must be based on rigorous reasoning.

Criteria for evaluating a test response¶

  • It is well valued if you answer the question posed with sufficient accuracy and correctness, and the answer is reasoned with solid arguments.

  • It is regularly valued if you answer the question posed with precision and correctness, but it is not well argued, or if, being well argued, you reach conclusions that are not entirely correct.

  • It is valued poorly if it does not answer the question posed, or is incorrect and inaccurate, or if, even if it is correct, it relies on fallacious arguments.

Work delivery¶

In general, activity reports will be sent by email to the teacher (they will not be uploaded to eGela). An identifier will be assigned to each group that will include the initials of the surnames. For example, if a group is composed of Etxeberria, Garcia and Gonzalez, the group identifier will be EGG. When you constitute the group, send an initial message with the composition of the group and the teacher will respond with the identifier.

Respect the following rules:

  • The message with the deliverable can be sent from any of the addresses of the group members, with copy (CC) to the rest of the members. For all communication about the same deliverable, please always use the option “answer all”.

  • The email “Subject” will include:

    • DS 2020-21

    • Group identifier

    • Report activity identifier

    Example: DS 2020-21 EGG A1.1

  • The attached documents (report, source code, etc.) will always include the names of all group members.

  • If it is a report, it can be either in plain text or in html in the body of the email itself. If, due to its length or format, you believe that inserting it in the message is not adequate, attach it in pdf with the same name (example: DS_2020-21_EGG_A1.1.pdf). Never attach “proprietary” formats, such as .doc or .docx. If it’s code, attach it in a .zip file.

It is important to follow these rules to facilitate the search, management and evaluation of the work by the teacher.
